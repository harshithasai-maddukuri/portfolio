You save a snippet with a title and a language tag. Search filters by title or language as you type, and every snippet has a one-click copy button. Each user also gets a public page at /u/username.

A learning platform has three very different users. Students enroll and submit work, instructors build and manage courses, and admins oversee everything. Each one needs to see and change only what's theirs.

The backend is organized into routes and data-access objects for courses, modules, users, and enrollments, with full create, read, update, and delete on each. Every request is checked against the user's role before it touches data.

Students and courses are many-to-many: one student takes many courses, and one course has many students.

I modeled that with a dedicated enrollments collection and used Mongoose populate() to join it back to users and courses, which keeps enrollment logic in one place instead of duplicating it in both records.

A working, deployed platform with the frontend on Vercel and the backend on Render, configured with separate environment variables for local and production and Git-based branch workflows.

People often describe how they're feeling in public posts long before they ask for help. We wanted to measure, honestly, how well classic NLP methods can recognize those signals.

My contributions

Model evaluation, stratified 5-fold cross-validation, confusion matrices, ROC and learning curves, error analysis, the ablation study, and the report and presentation.

TF-IDF turns each post into weighted word features, and SMOTE creates synthetic examples of the smaller classes so the model doesn't just learn to guess the majority. We compared classifiers across 60 configurations and Random Forest came out on top.

Partway through, we found label information leaking into the features, which made results look better than they really were.

We fixed the pipeline and reran all 60 configurations from scratch. Every number on this page comes from the corrected run.

Random Forest reached 84.98% accuracy and 0.8371 macro F1, stable across 5-fold cross-validation. I report macro F1 next to accuracy because with uneven classes, accuracy alone can hide a model that ignores the smaller groups.

Signature verification with Siamese networks

Deep learning research, undergraduate · 2022

Published · IJFANS

A system that decides whether a signature is genuine by comparing it with a known sample. Our team of five built the full pipeline and published it in IJFANS in 2022.

91.6%accuracy
750signatures
30writers
PythonDeep learningCNNDjangoJava
Read the paper ↗

Forged signatures are a real fraud risk, and checking them by eye is slow and inconsistent. An automated checker has to be careful in both directions: never accept a forgery, and don't reject the real person.

Uploadweb interface
→
Preprocessclean, normalize
→
Siamese CNNcontrastive loss
→
Decisiondistance vs. threshold

A Siamese network runs two signatures through the same CNN and measures how far apart they are. Trained with contrastive loss, it learns to pull genuine pairs together and push forgeries apart. A threshold on that distance makes the final call.

Where you set the threshold is a trade-off. Too loose and forgeries get through. Too strict and genuine signers get rejected.

We measured false accept and false reject rates to choose a threshold that balances the two.

The model reached 91.6% accuracy on a dataset of 750 signatures from 30 people, and works writer-independently, meaning it can verify signatures from people it never saw during training. The paper, "A Siamese Network based Writer Independent Offline Signature Verification," was published in IJFANS Vol. 11, No. 12 (2022).
